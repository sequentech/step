The voting system protects your vote on its servers and while it is sent. It
cannot protect what happens on your device or around you. That part is up to
you. This page says what you need and what you must do.

## What you need

- **Your login details.** The election organizer gives them to you before the
  election period, together with instructions on how to log in.
- **The address of the voting portal.** The election organizer gives it to you.
- **The election period.** The election organizer announces when voting starts
  and ends. You can vote only during that time.
- **The election organizer's instructions.** Before the election period, the
  election organizer tells you what the login page looks like, how many times
  you may vote (the vote you cast last counts), that you can check your ballot
  with "Audit ballot", where the verification data will be published and how
  to check with it that your vote was counted, and whom to contact about a
  security problem. Follow these instructions.
- **A device with a current web browser.** The browser must run the voting
  portal's scripts, because your vote is encrypted in the browser. Do not block
  scripts for the voting portal.

If you have not received your login details or the address before the election
period, contact the election organizer.

## Keep your login details secret

:::warning

**Your login details are yours alone.** Anyone who has them can vote in your
name.

- Never tell anyone your login details, and never write them where others can
  read them.
- Never let anyone else log in or vote for you, and never log in or vote for
  anyone else.
- Nobody has a good reason to ask you for your password. Do not give it, even if
  someone asks by phone, email or message, or claims to be from the election
  organizer.
- If you think someone else knows your login details, or you have lost them,
  contact the election organizer at once.

:::

## Vote where nobody can watch you

:::warning

**Your vote is secret only if nobody watches you vote.**

- Vote alone, where nobody can see your screen. This includes family members,
  colleagues and friends.
- Do not share your screen, do not record it, and do not vote during a video
  call.
- Do not take photos or screenshots of your selections, and do not let anyone
  take them.
- Do not let anyone tell you how to vote or check how you voted.
- Follow any further instructions your election organizer gives you about voting
  unobserved.

:::

If someone watched or pressured you while you voted, tell the election
organizer. If your election allows you to vote again, you can then vote again
where nobody watches you (see [Voting Again](08-voting_again.md)).

## Use a device and a browser you trust

Your device and your browser show you the ballot, encrypt your vote and send
it. Use only ones you trust:

- Use your own device if you can. Avoid shared or public computers.
- Keep the operating system and the browser up to date with security updates,
  and use the malware protection of your device.
- Do not install or use browser extensions you do not trust. An extension can
  read and change what the page shows you.
- Close programs that let other people see or control your screen, such as
  remote-control or screen-sharing programs, before you start.
- A private (incognito) window of your browser is a good choice: the browser
  forgets the page data when you close it.

:::warning

**Stop if your device or browser behaves strangely.** Examples: unexpected
pop-ups, pages that look different from what the election organizer described,
requests to install something, or a ballot that changes by itself. Do not cast
your vote on that device. Use another device you trust, and tell the election
organizer.

:::

## Check that you are on the real voting portal

A fake page can look like the voting portal. Before you log in, check that you
are on the real one:

1. Open the voting portal only with the address your election organizer gave
   you. Type it in yourself, or use a link from a message you know comes from
   the election organizer. Do not follow links in unexpected messages.
2. Check that the address bar of the browser shows exactly that address. Watch
   for small differences, such as a changed letter or an added word.
3. Check that the browser shows a secure connection (in most browsers, a lock
   symbol or a similar sign next to the address) and no warning.
4. Do the same checks on the login page of the authentication service: it must
   be the page your election organizer described.

:::danger

**Never accept a certificate or security warning.** The real voting portal and
ballot verifier never cause one. If the browser warns that the connection is
not secure or that the certificate is not valid, stop: do not continue, do not
enter your login details, do not select any option that lets you proceed
anyway, and do not try another address or link instead. Close the page and
report the warning to the election organizer.

:::

## Plan your time

- Start well before the end of the election period. A vote that is not cast
  when the election period ends cannot be cast any more.
- Have a few minutes without interruption. If you are inactive for 15 minutes,
  your session ends and you must log in again (see
  [Your Voting Session](09-voting_session.md)).
- If you want to check after the election that your vote was counted, copy
  your encrypted ballot from the ballot locator before the end of the election
  period (see
  [Checking That Your Vote Was Stored and Counted](07-checking_storage.md)).
